## What Is Sovereign Syndicate?

*Sovereign Syndicate* is a narrative-driven RPG heavily inspired by *Disco Elysium*, released in 2024 by Common Herring Studios. Set in a reimagined Victorian London where mythological races coexist with steam-and-gear technology, the game follows three protagonists whose paths initially seem unrelated but eventually converge:

- **Atticus Daley** – A minotaur struggling with alcoholism who wakes from a bender with a mysterious voice called the Old Crone in his head, only to stumble onto the trail of the mother he believed abandoned him.
- **Clara Reed** – An American expatriate working in the world's oldest profession, desperate to escape London but forced to scramble for money when law enforcement blocks her exit.
- **Theodore "Teddy" Redgrave** – A dwarven monster hunter who patrols the city taking contracts, accompanied by his automaton companion Otto.

The game is available on [Steam and GOG](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/SovereignSyndicate), where the community has built out an extensive catalog of its narrative patterns.

## The Core TV Tropes Players Should Know

The community has identified dozens of tropes at work in *Sovereign Syndicate*. Here are the ones that most directly affect how you play and experience the story.

### Absurdly Spacious Sewer

Victorian London's sewers are remarkably roomy in this game. Atticus can explore them in his first chapter if you choose that path, and Teddy later returns for a contract job. The sewers serve as a recurring location that connects both characters' storylines.

**Player takeaway:** Don't skip the sewers with Atticus. Exploring them early gives you narrative context and items that enrich Teddy's later visit. The game rewards thoroughness.

### Addiction-Powered

This trope is downplayed but mechanically present. Certain vices—drinking being the most obvious—grant experience boosts to personality traits or provide hope. The game doesn't punish vice as purely negative; it offers tangible short-term benefits.

**Player takeaway:** Indulging a character's vices can be strategically useful for passing specific skill checks. However, community reports suggest these choices carry narrative weight, so consider the long-term story implications before leaning too heavily on this mechanic.

### Alternate Techline

The technology in *Sovereign Syndicate* isn't more advanced than ours—just different. Steam and gear-based innovations emerged along an alternate path, with some concepts appearing earlier than in our history. Human augmentation exists, including modifications that affect the brain. The lore attributes this technological divergence to the mythical civilization of Atlantis.

**Player takeaway:** Pay attention to environmental storytelling. The tech isn't just window dressing; it informs which skill checks are available and how characters perceive the world around them.

### The Artful Dodger

Atticus befriends a young girl named Annika, who serves as a kid sidekick during his investigation into missing orphans. This trope signals the game's Dickensian influences and reinforces Atticus's role as someone who exists on society's margins.

**Player takeaway:** Annika provides access to information and pathways Atticus couldn't reach alone. Engage with her dialogue fully—she often hints at solutions to obstacles.

### Classical Cyclops

Cyclops are one of the prominent races in the setting. Two types exist: the Polyphemus variety, who live and work in civilized London (often in scientific fields), and a much larger, isolated species that eats people. Cyclops have also evolved to see in the dark.

**Player takeaway:** When encountering cyclops characters, remember their dark-vision capability. This racial trait can influence dialogue options and skill checks in dimly lit environments.

### Clock Punk Meets Steampunk

While primarily Steampunk, the game layers in significant Clock Punk elements. Gear and clock-based technology appears throughout the world—wristwatch vending machines, mechanical pigeons for mail delivery, and the cogs Teddy needs to upgrade Otto. The lore implies a clock technology age preceded the current steam era.

**Player takeaway:** Collect gears and cogs whenever possible. They're not just thematic; they're a functional resource for upgrading Teddy's automaton companion.

## How the Game's Major Themes Shape Gameplay

Beyond individual tropes, *Sovereign Syndicate* is built around several interlocking themes that directly affect mechanics and player choices. Understanding these themes helps you navigate the game more effectively.

### Nature of the Self

Each character's inner monologue features multiple voices representing different facets of their personality. No single voice is the "real" one—all of them compose the whole. Aspects of each inner voice appear in spoken dialogue, and balancing these sides is central to each character's arc.

This manifests mechanically through skill checks and traits. Focusing heavily on one personality aspect opens certain options while closing others.

| Personality Focus | Potential Benefit | Potential Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Aggressive voices | Intimidation checks, direct solutions | Missed diplomatic options, damaged relationships |
| Cautious voices | Avoiding danger, gathering information | Slower progress, missed opportunities |
| Empathetic voices | Building trust, uncovering secrets | Vulnerability to manipulation |
| Cynical voices | Seeing through deception, self-protection | Alienating allies, losing hope |

### Perspective

Each protagonist interacts with the same world differently based on their lived experience. Atticus navigates sewers looking for strangers and things to steal. Teddy moves through the same spaces encountering familiar faces and applying technical expertise. Your drift toward optimism or cynicism literally changes the dialogue options available to you.

**Player takeaway:** There is no single "correct" perspective. The game is designed to reward different approaches, and replaying with a different emotional tilt reveals new content.

### Social and Scientific Progress

Set during the industrial revolution, the game constantly interrogates the relationship between scientific advancement and social stagnation. Progress comes with costs, and those costs drive many of the conflicts you encounter.

**Player takeaway:** Dialogue choices that engage with this theme often yield the most narrative insight. Characters who discuss the tensions between progress and humanity tend to reveal crucial story information.

### Prejudice

Prejudice is not a background element—it's a core mechanical and narrative force. Atticus grapples with his distrust of centaurs and the pain of being perceived as a monster himself. Using a human disguise in chapter one can actually cost him hope, as it means hiding his true self to gain respect. Teddy carries traumatic cynicism from warfare. Clara confronts sexism and sexualization constantly.

The plight of the werewolves serves as a major story element tied to these themes.

**Player takeaway:** Choices that confront prejudice head-on rather than avoid it tend to yield more meaningful character development, even when they carry short-term costs.

## Character-Specific Tropes and Practical Strategies

Each protagonist embodies and subverts multiple tropes simultaneously. Here's how to leverage that knowledge during gameplay.

### Atticus Daley: The Reluctant Monster

Atticus combines the "monster with a heart of gold" trope with the "addicted protagonist" archetype. His minotaur nature makes him a target of prejudice, and his drinking is both a coping mechanism and a gameplay tool.

- Prioritize building rapport with Annika early—she unlocks investigative paths
- Consider whether the hope loss from using the human disguise is worth the social access
- Use the sewers as a safe space to explore his criminal skill set without immediate consequences

### Clara Reed: The Woman With a Plan

Clara embodies the "sex worker with dreams of escape" trope, but the game subverts it by making her circumstances feel systemic rather than personal. Her need for money isn't a character flaw—it's a structural barrier enforced by law enforcement.

- Focus on dialogue options that build alliances—Clara's strength lies in social manipulation
- Track which characters can provide financial opportunities versus narrative ones
- Her cynicism is earned; leaning into it opens pragmatic options

### Teddy Redgrave: The Battle-Scarred Hunter

Teddy fits the "grizzled monster hunter" archetype, but his trauma from warfare adds depth. His relationship with Otto provides a mechanical anchor—keeping the automaton upgraded is essential.

| Priority | Action | Reason |

| High | Engage with technical skill checks | Teddy's unique strength |
| Medium | Explore sewers during his contract | Reveals connections to Atticus's story |
| Medium | Confront wartime trauma in dialogue | Unlocks deeper character development |
| Low | Avoid unnecessary combat | Teddy's cynicism can close off diplomatic paths |

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is "Sovereign Tower" the same game as Sovereign Syndicate?

Yes—players searching for "Sovereign Tower tv tropes" are almost certainly looking for information about *Sovereign Syndicate*. The similar naming has caused confusion in community discussions, but the TV Tropes page and all official sources refer to the game as *Sovereign Syndicate* by Common Herring Studios.

### Do the three characters' stories actually connect?

They do. While Atticus, Clara, and Teddy begin with seemingly separate storylines, the narratives converge as the game progresses. Shared locations like the sewers and overlapping themes of prejudice and progress serve as early connective tissue before the plots fully intertwine.

### How does the Addiction-Powered trope work mechanically?

Community reports indicate that certain vices—such as Atticus's drinking—provide experience boosts to personality traits or grant hope. The mechanic is downplayed compared to how some games handle substance use, but it offers real short-term gameplay benefits. The long-term narrative consequences are less fully documented, so players should weigh immediate gains against potential story outcomes.

### Should I play Disco Elysium first to understand this game?

Not necessarily, but it helps. *Sovereign Syndicate* is heavily inspired by *Disco Elysium* in terms of narrative structure and the inner-voice personality system. If you've played *Disco Elysium*, you'll recognize the design philosophy immediately. If you haven't, the game stands on its own—you'll just be experiencing its innovations without the context of its inspiration.
