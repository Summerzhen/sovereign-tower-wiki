## Core Gameplay Overview

Sovereign Tower blends several genres—visual novel, interactive fiction, role-playing, and simulation—into a Round Table management experience. You play as the Sovereign of a magical tower, guided by your advisor Arlin, and your primary job is to keep your kingdom running by managing Knights, resolving subject requests, and balancing competing faction interests.

### Your Daily Routine as Sovereign

Each day follows a structured rhythm that determines your priorities:

- **Morning**: Address your subjects and hear their problems. Requests range from mundane tasks like gardening to more dangerous assignments such as exorcisms.
- **Afternoon**: Manage your Round Table by assigning Knights to the quests you accepted earlier.
- **Evening**: Review quest results, handle Knight grievances, and make decisions that affect faction affinity.

Every choice you make ripples outward, shaping relationships with individuals, collectives, and neighboring rulers. There's no single "correct" path—your legacy is defined by the cumulative weight of your decisions.

## Knight Management: The Heart of the Game

Your Knights are the engine that keeps the kingdom functioning, but they're also your biggest source of headaches. Each Knight comes with a distinct personality, questionable morals, and an ego that demands constant attention.

### Understanding Knight Stats

Knights possess five core stats that determine their suitability for different quests:

| Stat | Best For | Example Quest Type |
|------|----------|-------------------|
| Strength | Physical confrontations and labor | Monster slaying, construction |
| Agility | Stealth and quick action | Reconnaissance, theft recovery |
| Charisma | Social interactions and persuasion | Diplomacy, negotiations |
| Magic | Supernatural challenges | Exorcisms, enchantment removal |
| Wit | Puzzle-solving and strategy | Investigations, scholarly disputes |

Matching the right Knight to the right quest significantly reduces the chance of failure. As the game's own advisor hints, sending a low-Charisma Knight like Ursula on a diplomatic mission is a recipe for disaster.

### Keeping Knights Satisfied

Knights aren't passive tools—they have needs, opinions, and the ability to walk away from your court entirely. Key factors in Knight satisfaction include:

- **Listening to their advice and grievances** during council sessions
- **Assigning quests that align with their strengths** to avoid embarrassing failures
- **Exploring romance options** with interested Knights, which deepens loyalty
- **Using The Intendant's intelligence** to uncover secrets that help you navigate interpersonal drama

If a Knight becomes too dissatisfied, they may leave permanently. Sometimes that's actually beneficial if their personality clashes with the rest of your court, but losing a high-stat Knight always stings.

### Quest Outcomes

When Knights return from their assignments, you'll receive a debrief with one of three possible results:

- **Critical Success**: The Knight excelled beyond expectations, often yielding bonus rewards or improved faction standing
- **Critical Failure**: Things went catastrophically wrong, potentially damaging relationships or losing resources
- **Unexpected Outcome**: The result diverges from the obvious success/failure binary, sometimes opening new story paths

These outcomes aren't purely random—Knight stats, quest suitability, and possibly hidden factors all play a role.

## Faction Affinity and Kingdom Balance

Maintaining your kingdom requires juggling the interests of six distinct factions:

- **Merchants** – Concerned with trade and economic growth
- **Mystics** – Focused on magical and supernatural matters
- **Scholars** – Prioritize knowledge and research
- **Nobles** – Care about tradition, status, and political power
- **The People** – Everyday citizens with practical needs
- **The Treasury** – The financial backbone of your operations

Every decision you make shifts your standing with one or more factions. You can try to keep everyone moderately happy, or you can go all-in on specific alliances while backstabbing others. Both approaches are viable, but extreme imbalances create consequences that may force difficult choices later.

## The Time-Rewind Mechanic: Your Safety Net

Perhaps the most distinctive feature in Sovereign Tower is the **Demon in the Crypts**, who can turn back time. If a decision leads to disaster—or even just a suboptimal outcome—you can rewind with all the knowledge you've gained intact.

This mechanic transforms the game from a simple management sim into a strategic puzzle. Key considerations for using time rewind:

- **You retain meta-knowledge** from previous timelines, allowing you to make informed choices
- **Alternate decisions may unlock completely different story branches** you'd never see otherwise
- **The game encourages experimentation** rather than punishing failure
- **Secrets uncovered in one timeline remain relevant** when you rewind, making exploration valuable even if things go wrong

### When to Consider Rewinding

| Situation | Rewind? | Reasoning |
|-----------|---------|-----------|
| Critical Failure on a major quest | Likely | Prevents lasting damage to faction standing or Knight morale |
| Knight leaves court unexpectedly | Depends | If the Knight was valuable, yes; if they were problematic, maybe not |
| Unexpected Outcome opens new path | No | These often lead to unique content worth exploring |
| Faction affinity drops dangerously | Likely | Severe imbalances can cascade into bigger problems |
