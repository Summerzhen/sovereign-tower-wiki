## Getting Started in Sovereign Tower

Sovereign Tower drops you into the role of a mysterious leper who discovers a legendary silver key and claims an abandoned tower in the land of Bratannia. This story-driven kingdom manager tasks you with balancing faction satisfaction, managing a growing roster of knights, and making dialogue choices that shape your sovereign's personality. This Sovereign Tower demo guide covers everything you need to know to navigate your first several cycles effectively.

The demo is surprisingly deep—spanning multiple cycles with dozens of quests, character interactions, and a climactic challenge from the Dragon Knight. Understanding the core systems early prevents costly mistakes like losing knights or tanking faction approval.

## Understanding the Core Systems

### Faction Satisfaction Balance

Four factions determine the stability of your rule: Merchants, Scholars, Nobles, and the People. Each starts at a neutral level of five. Your goal is to keep them balanced—neither too low nor excessively high. Extreme values in either direction can trigger problems or even threaten your throne.

Faction satisfaction shifts based on:

- **Dialogue choices** during audiences
- **Quest outcomes** (success or failure)
- **Spending decisions** (funding road improvements, purifying cursed land)
- **Which requests you accept or decline**

Refusing a request often lowers the related faction's satisfaction, so think carefully before turning subjects away.

### The Cycle System

Time progresses in cycles rather than a real-time clock. Each cycle, you:

1. Hold morning audiences with petitioners
2. Assign knights to quests at the Round Table
3. End the cycle, triggering quest resolutions and tax income
4. Unlock new rooms and interact with tower residents

Tax income arrives each cycle from your controlled locations on the map. Damaged or destroyed locations stop generating revenue until repaired.

### Sovereign Personality Stats

Your choices accumulate into personality traits: Audacity, Wisdom, Kindness, and Tyranny. These appear to influence narrative outcomes and how characters perceive you. Based on player experience, choosing compassionate options builds Kindness, while pragmatic or manipulative choices build Wisdom or Tyranny respectively.

## Knight Management Essentials

Knights are your primary tool for completing quests beyond the tower walls. Each knight has distinct stats, preferences, and personality quirks. Mismanaging them leads to failed quests, lost affinity, or even permanent death.

### Knight Stats and Quest Compatibility

| Stat | Effect |
|------|--------|
| Strength | Physical challenges, combat, hunting |
| Agility | Speed-based tasks, tracking, evasion |
| Charisma | Diplomacy, negotiation, social quests |
| Luck | Random outcome improvement |
| Magic | Mystical challenges, enchanted encounters |
| Wits | Investigation, puzzle-solving, research |

Quests display a difficulty wheel showing which stats matter. Match knights whose strengths align with those requirements. A knight with no Magic sent on a magic-requiring quest will likely fail or take heavy damage.

### Knight Preferences Matter

Knights have explicit likes and dislikes. Sending them on preferred quests raises their affinity; assigning unwanted tasks lowers it. Check each knight's backstory before deployment.

| Knight | Prefers | Dislikes |
|--------|---------|----------|
| Angelica | Tracking, nature quests, cute creatures | Heavy combat, scary situations |
| Goberto | Monster hunting, fighting creatures | Delicate tasks, anything requiring finesse |
| Gwendon | Jousting, social events, showing off | Manual labor, tracking, carrying heavy items |
| Ursula | Spooky places, exotic/unusual quests | Magic and mysticism (ironically), diplomatic errands |
| Gideon | Diplomatic missions, social intrigue | Unclear—seems versatile but dramatic |

### Armor and Knight Survival

Knights have armor points that deplete during quests. When armor reaches zero, the knight dies permanently—there is no revival in the demo. Repair armor at the Forge each cycle (one repair per cycle is allowed). Prioritize repairing knights with low armor before sending them out.

This is the most critical lesson from the demo: **Always check armor before assigning quests.** A single oversight cost Goberto his life during recorded playthroughs.

## Room Unlocks and Tower Progression

The Lady of the Tower awakens as you progress, granting access to new rooms. Each room provides unique functionality.

### Available Rooms

- **Gallery / Map Room**: View the world map, check active requests, and send aid to villages. The map also shows location details and tax income values.
- **Intendency**: Run by Elena, this room provides rumors about your knights—useful for understanding their quirks and hidden traits. She also offers gossip that can hint at future events.
- **Forge**: Operated by Karina the blacksmith. Repair knight armor (one per cycle) and purchase relics that boost knight stats. Relics can be equipped at the Round Table.
- **Witch Tower**: Unlocked after inviting Witch Belladona (triggered through a map request). She sells potions for various effects.

Room unlocks appear to be gated by story progression. The Intendency unlocks first, then the Forge, and finally the Witch Tower. You cannot unlock all rooms simultaneously—choose based on immediate needs.

## Quest Assignment Strategy

### Reading Quest Requirements

Each quest displays:

- **Duration**: How many cycles the knight will be away
- **Difficulty wheel**: Visual representation of required stats
- **Damage indicator**: Estimated armor loss
- **Rewards**: Money, faction favor, items, or mounts

Quests with magic requirements are uncommon early on. Without a magic-capable knight like Ursula, skip these until she joins your roster.

### Mount and Equipment Management

Quest rewards sometimes include mounts like Paul the Donkey (+1 Strength, -2 Charisma, reduces cycle duration) or horses (+Charisma). Equip these at the Round Table before assigning knights to quests.

Relics purchased from the Forge provide stat boosts. A Royal Sword grants Charisma, while a lake-surfaced magic sword offers Strength and Agility at a Charisma cost. Choose equipment that complements the knight's role.

### Common Quest Types

- **Hunting/Monster Slaying**: Requires Strength, sometimes Agility
- **Diplomatic/Negotiation**: Requires Charisma and Wits
- **Investigation/Research**: Requires Wits and sometimes Magic
- **Retrieval**: Mixed requirements depending on the item

## The Dragon Knight Challenge

Around cycle four, the Dragon Knight arrives and challenges your right to rule. He sets target objectives:

| Objective | Target | Typical Starting Value |
|-----------|--------|----------------------|
| Merchant Satisfaction | 8 | ~9 |
| People Satisfaction | 14 | ~9 |
| Money | 300 | ~249 |

You have four cycles to meet these targets. If achieved, you must then send four knights to battle the Dragon Knight. The demo appears designed so that reaching four knights in time is extremely tight—losing even one knight to poor armor management makes the challenge nearly impossible.

If you fail the Dragon Knight's challenge, the Time Traveling Demon appears, offering to rewind your decisions. This seems to be the demo's built-in retry mechanic, hinting at a larger role for the demon in the full game.

## Key Tips for Demo Success

- **Repair armor every cycle** at the Forge—don't skip this
- **Read knight backstories** before assigning quests to match preferences
- **Decline quests you can't handle** rather than sending ill-suited knights
- **Invest in infrastructure** like road improvements for long-term faction gains
- **Check the map room** between audiences for new requests triggered by conversations
- **Balance faction spending**—don't drain your treasury on early requests
- **Recruit every knight** who offers to join; you need a full roster
- **Avoid sending knights on magic quests** until you have a magic-capable recruit
- **Listen to rumors** in the Intendency for insights about knight behavior
- **Don't let Gwendon's arrogance fool you**—his jousting preference is genuine, but he's fragile emotionally and may leave if offended

## FAQ

### How do I unlock the Witch Tower in the Sovereign Tower demo?

The Witch Tower unlocks after you trigger a map request to invite Witch Belladona. This request appears after certain conversations in the throne room mention a witch. Pay the 10 money invitation fee, and she arrives the next cycle. Once she's at the tower, speak to the Lady of the Tower in the Gallery to unlock the room.

### Can knights die permanently in Sovereign Tower?

Yes. When a knight's armor points reach zero during a quest, they die permanently. The demo shows this with a coffin icon replacing the knight's portrait. There is no revival mechanic available. Always repair armor at the Forge before sending knights on dangerous missions—this is the most important rule in this Sovereign Tower demo guide.

### What happens if I fail the Dragon Knight challenge?

If you fail to meet the resource targets or lose the battle, the Time Traveling Demon appears and offers to rewind your decisions. This demon exists within a padlock in the tower and serves as a narrative retry mechanic. The full extent of the demon's powers will likely be explored in the complete game, but in the demo, this encounter marks the endpoint.

### How do I know which knight to assign to a quest?

Check the quest's difficulty wheel for required stats, then compare against each knight's stat profile. Also review the knight's backstory for preferences—sending them on quests they enjoy raises affinity, while unwanted assignments lower it. If a knight expresses displeasure when assigned ("I'm not pleased by your choice"), consider reassigning to someone better suited.
