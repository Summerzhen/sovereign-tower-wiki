## Quick Answer

Knight loyalty appears to be separate from raw quest ability. A knight can be capable and still dislike an assignment, so manage loyalty as a long-term route resource while using stats and traits to win quests.

## Loyalty vs Ability

| System | Main Question |
|---|---|
| Loyalty | Will this knight stay committed to the ruler and route? |
| Affinity | Is the relationship improving or getting strained? |
| Quest preference | Does this assignment make the knight happier or less satisfied? |
| Quest ability | Can the knight meet the requirement and survive the outcome? |

## How to Protect Loyalty

1. Avoid repeated disliked assignments unless the quest reward is worth it.
2. Use preferred or neutral quests after a loyalty loss.
3. Watch for warning dialogue that suggests a knight may leave.
4. Do not confuse a successful quest with a happy knight.
5. Track loyalty changes beside stats, traits, and outcomes.

## Loyalty Risk Table

| Situation | Risk | Safer Response |
|---|---|---|
| High-stakes quest, disliked type | Loyalty drop but possible success | Send the best fit, then repair loyalty |
| Low-stakes disliked quest | Unnecessary relationship damage | Use another capable knight |
| Repeated failures | Route instability | Rewind, change assignment, or upgrade gear |
| Departure warning | Possible roster loss | Pause risky assignments and rebuild trust |

## FAQ

### Can a knight leave because of low loyalty?

Treat departure as a real risk when warning text appears, but verify the exact threshold or trigger before publishing precise numbers.

### Is loyalty the same as affinity?

Not necessarily. Use "loyalty" for commitment and departure risk, and "affinity" for relationship or satisfaction tracking unless confirmed text says otherwise.

### Should I always choose the loyal option?

Not always. Sometimes quest success matters more, especially when failure can damage the route, gear, or roster.
