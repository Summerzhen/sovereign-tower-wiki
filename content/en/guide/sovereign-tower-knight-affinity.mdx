## Quick Answer

Quest preference is not the same as quest ability. Sending a knight to a disliked quest can lower affinity, but it does not automatically make the quest fail. Stats, traits, equipment, and requirements matter more for the actual result.

## Affinity vs Quest Success

| System | Affects |
|---|---|
| Affinity | Dialogue, satisfaction, relationship progress, risk of a knight leaving |
| Quest preference | Affinity gain or loss from assignment type |
| Stats | Whether the knight can meet quest requirements |
| Traits | Special modifiers, hidden risks, or unexpected outcomes |

## How to Manage Affinity

Prioritize quest success when the stakes are high, then repair affinity with better dialogue, preferred assignments, meals, or relationship choices later. A small satisfaction loss is easier to recover from than a failed quest, damaged armor, or a dead knight.

## Common Misread

Players often assume a disliked quest means a bad assignment. In practice, a knight can dislike a quest type and still be highly capable if the stat and trait match is strong.
