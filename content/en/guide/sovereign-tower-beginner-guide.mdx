## Getting Started in Sovereign Tower

Sovereign Tower throws a lot of systems at you right from the first cycle. Between faction reputations, knight attributes, and branching dialogue choices, it is easy to feel overwhelmed before you even finish your first quest. This Sovereign Tower beginner guide breaks down every core mechanic introduced in Cycle 1 so you can make confident decisions from the very start.

The game revolves around a deceptively simple loop: listen to the people who visit your tower, decide whether to help them, and assign knights to handle the problems you take on. How you manage those knights—and how your choices shift your reputation with four distinct factions—determines the options available to you later. Understanding these systems early prevents costly mistakes.

## Understanding the Core UI and Faction Reputation

When you begin Cycle 1, the top-left corner of your screen displays the information you will reference constantly:

| UI Element | What It Tracks |
|---|---|
| Current Cycle | Which day or cycle you are on |
| Merchants Reputation | Standing with the merchant faction |
| Scholars Reputation | Standing with the scholar faction |
| Nobles Reputation | Standing with the noble faction |
| People Reputation | Standing with the common people |
| Available Funds | Your current gold reserves |

Every choice you make can raise or lower your standing with one or more factions. Increasing satisfaction with a faction unlocks new dialogue options and opens up fresh items in their shops. Neglecting a faction can close off paths entirely.

### Sovereign Archetypes Explained

On the right side of the screen, the Sovereign Menu lets you track your reputation alongside your Sovereign Archetypes. Your archetype shifts based on the decisions you make throughout the game. Each archetype has three thresholds, and hitting a threshold unlocks new dialogue choices. Some dialogue options only appear when one archetype is significantly higher than another, which means your build matters for more than just flavor—it gates content.

Because the game features branching outcomes and multiple endings, there is no single correct archetype path. However, concentrating on one or two archetypes rather than spreading thin tends to unlock threshold rewards faster in the early cycles.

## Knight Management: Your Most Important System

Knights are the engine of your progress. You assign them to quests, and their statistics, attributes, and party size determine whether you succeed or fail. The glossary tab in the Sovereign Menu stores details on every servant and knight you have recruited.

### Knight Stats and Leveling

Knights earn experience points by completing quests. When a knight levels up, you allocate one point to a statistic of your choice. Early on, you will not have many knights, so making each level-up count matters. Pay attention to the stat requirements of the quests you plan to tackle and spec accordingly.

### Attributes and Hidden Traits

Every knight has attributes that influence their behavior during quests. Some attributes are visible from the start, while others are hidden until you discover them through specific interactions. For example, praising Angelica's cat reveals her hidden "Animal Lover" trait. Discovering these hidden attributes gives you better information for quest assignment.

Attributes can have positive or negative effects depending on the quest. A knight who is Kind-Hearted might excel at one task but struggle at another. Always read the quest description carefully and compare it against your knight's known attributes before assigning them.

### Affinity: The Loyalty System

Affinity measures how a knight currently feels about you as the Sovereign. This is separate from quest performance—it is shaped by your dialogue choices and how you treat each knight day to day.

- **High affinity** unlocks new dialogue options with that knight, deepening your relationship and sometimes revealing useful information.
- **Low affinity** can cause a knight to leave the Round Table entirely, removing them from your roster.

Knight preferences—like whether they enjoy cleaning or certain tasks—affect affinity but do not impact quest performance directly. Use preferences to build rapport, and avoid choices that alienate your knights.

| Affinity Level | Effect |
|---|---|
| High | Unlocks new dialogue, strengthens loyalty |
| Medium | Default state, no special effects |
| Low | Risk of knight abandoning the Round Table |

## Exploring the Tower: Rooms and Resources

Your tower is more than a backdrop—it is a functional hub that grows as you recruit servants. Early in Cycle 1, you meet the Lady of the Tower, who can unlock new rooms as you bring in specific servants. Expanding your tower opens up mechanical advantages, so recruiting servants should be a priority whenever possible.

### The Map Room

The Map Room lets you issue orders that affect the world outside your tower. From here you can:

- **Fix damaged areas** that were harmed by failed quests, restoring them for future use.
- **Invite people for an audience**, giving you more visitors and potential quest opportunities.
- **Recall knights who were turned away**, offering second chances at valuable party members.

Checking the Map Room each cycle is a good habit. Neglecting damaged areas can compound problems later, and missed audiences mean missed quests and rewards.

### The Gallery and the Lady of the Tower

The Lady of the Tower resides in the gallery. She unlocks rooms based on which servants you have recruited. Planning your servant recruitment around the rooms you want most can shape your early strategy. Community reports also note that you can romance the statue, though the long-term consequences of this choice remain a matter of player experience rather than confirmed mechanics.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How many knights can I have at the Round Table?

At the start of the game, you can have up to 6 knights at the Round Table. As you progress and unlock new rooms through the Lady of the Tower by recruiting servants, this capacity may expand.

### What happens if a knight's affinity gets too low?

Low affinity can cause a knight to leave the Round Table entirely. Once they leave, you lose access to that knight unless you use the Map Room to invite them back. Building affinity through dialogue choices and respecting knight preferences is the best way to prevent this.

### How do I discover hidden knight attributes?

Hidden attributes are revealed through specific interactions with each knight. For example, praising Angelica's cat in the kitchen uncovers her Animal Lover trait. Talk to your knights regularly, pay attention to their preferences, and experiment with different dialogue options to uncover hidden traits that inform better quest assignments.

### Is there a wrong choice in this Sovereign Tower beginner guide's early decisions?

Not permanently wrong, no. The game is built around branching paths and multiple outcomes. Declining Angelica's cat, for instance, simply means you miss discovering her hidden attribute early. You can still progress, but you will have less information for quest planning. Every choice shifts your archetype and faction reputations, so treat early decisions as opportunities to learn the system rather than stress over perfection.
