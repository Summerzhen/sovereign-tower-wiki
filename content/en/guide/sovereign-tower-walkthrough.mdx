## Getting Started as the Sovereign

Sovereign Tower drops you into the role of a wandering leper chosen by the gods to rule a long-abandoned kingdom. This Sovereign Tower walkthrough covers everything you need to know about balancing factions, managing your knights, and surviving your first cycles on the throne. The game operates on a cycle-based system rather than a ticking clock, so you can take your time with decisions—but every choice still carries weight.

Your advisor Arlin greets you inside the tower and explains the basics. Listen to his full tutorial, as it covers the core loop: hold audiences with petitioners, assign knights to quests at the round table, and end your day to advance to the next cycle. Skipping the tutorial means missing critical context about knight preferences and quest mechanics.

Use this walkthrough as the route map, then verify details in the [Quest Database](/quests), [Knights Database](/knights), [Meals Guide](/systems/sovereign-tower-meals), and [All Endings guide](/guide/sovereign-tower-endings). Keeping those records linked makes it easier to separate confirmed requirements from route-specific observations.

## Understanding the Four Factions

Balancing four faction satisfaction scores is the heart of the game. Each group—Merchants, Scholars, Nobles, and the People—has a satisfaction rating that shifts based on your decisions. Let any faction drop too low or climb too high, and you risk instability or even being dethroned.

| Faction | What They Care About | Risk If Too Low |
|---------|---------------------|-----------------|
| Merchants | Trade routes, financial decisions, market stability | Lost income, destroyed locations |
| Scholars | Relics, knowledge quests, magical matters | Reduced quest options, lost relic access |
| Nobles | Prestige, duels, estate management | Political opposition, knight departures |
| People | Basic needs, charity, infrastructure | Revolts, location damage |

Early in the game, all four sit at a balanced five. Small decisions—like spending 15 money to purify cursed farmland—shift these values incrementally. Always check the preview on the left side of dialogue options before committing, as it shows which factions will be affected.

### Key Faction Tips

- Declining requests from a faction's representative lowers their satisfaction. If you can't help, weigh the cost of refusal.
- Spending money on infrastructure (like improving roads for 120 money) can boost multiple factions simultaneously.
- Some quests reward faction favor directly. Prioritize these when a group is trending downward.

## Knight Recruitment and Management

Knights are your primary tool for completing quests beyond the tower walls. Each knight has unique stats, personality traits, and quest preferences. Ignoring these preferences is the fastest way to lose affinity and eventually lose the knight entirely.

### Knight Stats Explained

Every knight has six core stats plus an armor rating:

| Stat | Effect on Quests |
|------|-----------------|
| Strength | Physical challenges, combat encounters |
| Agility | Speed-based tasks, dodging hazards |
| Charisma | Diplomatic missions, negotiations |
| Luck | Random outcomes, finding hidden items |
| Magic | Mystical quests, supernatural encounters |
| Wits | Puzzle-solving, investigation missions |

Armor acts as a health buffer. When a knight takes damage on a quest, they lose armor points. If armor reaches zero, the knight can die permanently. Always check armor levels before sending knights on dangerous missions, and repair armor at the forge between cycles.

### Known Knights and Their Preferences

Based on player experience with the demo, here are the knights you can recruit and what to keep in mind:

- **Lady Angelica of Clovermont**: Your first knight. High agility and luck, low combat stats. She enjoys forest-related tasks and likes cute creatures. Avoid sending her on magic-requiring quests.
- **Sir Goberto**: A boastful monster hunter with high strength. Claims to slay dragons but has a track record of failing against smaller threats. Heavy armor user who gets injured frequently.
- **Sir Gwendan of Villidor**: Extremely high charisma and ego. Enjoys duels and being the center of attention. Hates tasks he considers beneath him, and will leave the round table if you make decisions he finds dishonorable.
- **Ursula**: A wandering adventurer with magical abilities. Hates diplomacy but loves spooky, unusual quests. Her magic stat makes her essential for supernatural missions.
- **Gideon**: A dramatic, flamboyant knight who excels at diplomatic and social quests. Surprisingly effective despite his theatrical personality.

### The Affinity System

When you assign a knight to a quest they enjoy, their affinity increases. Force them into undesirable work, and affinity drops. Low affinity leads to knights leaving your court—sometimes dramatically. Gwendan, for example, will abandon you if you make choices that offend his sense of propriety.

Pay attention to the knight's reaction when you assign them. "I'm on it" means they're happy. "If you say so" or "I'm not pleased by your choice" means affinity will decrease.

## Quest Assignment Strategy

The round table is where you match knights to available quests. Each quest shows a difficulty wheel indicating which stats matter, an estimated duration, and rewards. Matching the right knight to the right quest is critical.

### Reading the Difficulty Wheel

The wheel displays icons corresponding to the stats needed for success. If a quest shows strength and magic icons, you need a knight with both. Sending a knight who lacks the required stats often results in failure—and potentially in the knight taking heavy armor damage or dying.

### Quest Duration and Knight Availability

Quests take one or more cycles to complete. A knight on a quest is unavailable until they return. Plan around this:

1. **Always keep at least one knight unassigned** for unexpected urgent quests.
2. **Check returning knights' armor** before sending them out again.
3. **Longer quests mean more risk**—send well-equipped knights with high relevant stats.

### Common Quest Types

| Quest Type | Key Stats | Best Knights |
|-----------|-----------|-------------|
| Hunting/Combat | Strength, Agility | Goberto (in theory) |
| Diplomacy/Negotiation | Charisma, Wits | Gideon, Gwendan |
| Investigation | Wits, Luck | Angelica |
| Magic/Supernatural | Magic, Wits | Ursula |
| Duels | Strength, Charisma | Gwendan |

## Tower Rooms and Unlocks

As you progress, the Lady of the Tower awakens and offers to restore rooms. Each new room provides functionality critical to your success.

### Room Unlock Order

1. **Intendency**: Unlocked first. The stewardess Elena provides rumors about your knights—useful for understanding their quirks and avoiding costly mistakes.
2. **Forge**: Unlocked after recruiting enough knights. Run by Karina the blacksmith, the forge lets you buy relics (equipment for knights) and repair armor. You can repair one piece of armor per cycle, so prioritize knights going on dangerous quests.
3. **Witch Tower**: Unlocked after inviting Witch Belladona to your court (costs 10 money via a map request). She sells potions that can aid your knights.

### The Gallery and Map Room

The gallery and map room let you view your kingdom, check location details, and find available requests. Locations generate tax income each cycle, and damaged locations stop producing money. Repair them promptly—restoring a destroyed location like Grest costs 150 money but is essential for maintaining your income stream.

## The Dragon Knight Challenge

Around cycle four, the Dragon Knight from Draovich appears and challenges your right to rule. This is a major story event with a deadline: you must meet specific resource targets by a target cycle (cycle eight in the demo) and then send knights to face him in combat.

### Target Requirements

| Resource | Target | Starting Value (Approximate) |
|----------|--------|------------------------------|
| Merchant Satisfaction | 8 | 9 |
| People Satisfaction | 14 | 9 |
| Money | 300 | 249 |

The people satisfaction target is the hardest to hit. Focus on quests and decisions that boost the People faction, and spend generously on their requests during audiences.

### The Confrontation

You need at least four knights to attempt the Dragon Knight encounter. If you've lost knights to death or departure, you may not have enough. The demo suggests this fight is extremely difficult even with a full roster, and failure leads to the intervention of the tower's demon—who offers the power to rewind time and undo your mistakes.

## The Time-Traveling Demon

When things go catastrophically wrong, a demon sealed in the tower offers help. Described as having the power of "forgiving and forgetting," this entity essentially allows you to retry from an earlier point, making different decisions. In the demo, the demon appears after the Dragon Knight defeat, suggesting this mechanic activates at major failure points in the full game.

## Managing Your Sovereign's Personality

Your dialogue choices shape your sovereign's personality across four traits: Audacity, Wisdom, Tyranny, and Kindness. These are tracked separately from faction satisfaction and affect available dialogue options and story outcomes.

- **Wisdom**: Gained through measured, strategic choices (like offering reduced sentences for cooperation).
- **Audacity**: Gained through bold or unconventional actions (like drinking Ursula's spider-porridge cocktail).
- **Kindness**: Gained through compassionate decisions (allowing refugees into the tower).
- **Tyranny**: Gained through harsh or cruel choices.

Balance matters here too. Making Gwendan happy might require different personality traits than helping Angelica.

## FAQ

**How do I unlock new rooms in Sovereign Tower?**
Visit the Gallery after completing audiences each cycle. The Lady of the Tower offers room unlocks when you've met the prerequisites. The Intendency comes first, followed by the Forge once you have enough knights, and the Witch Tower after inviting Belladona through a map request costing 10 money.

**What happens if a knight's armor reaches zero?**
The knight dies permanently. This is why checking armor levels and repairing at the forge between cycles is essential. You can only repair one knight's armor per cycle, so prioritize whoever is heading into the most dangerous quest next.

**How many knights do I need for the Dragon Knight encounter?**
You need at least four knights assigned to the confrontation. If knights have died or left your round table, you won't be able to attempt the fight. This Sovereign Tower walkthrough recommends keeping knight affinity high and armor repaired to avoid this situation.

**Can I undo bad decisions in Sovereign Tower?**
Yes—the time-traveling demon sealed in the tower can rewind your progress after major failures. This mechanic appears to activate at critical story failure points, giving you a chance to make different choices on your next attempt.
