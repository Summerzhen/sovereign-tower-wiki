## Quick Answer

Use the Servants and Intendant route to track internal tower management. Record servant names, Intendant dialogue, annex references, resource effects, and any administrative choice that changes quest access, loyalty, or future risk.

## Route Overview

| Route Point | Working Plan |
|---|---|
| Primary goal | Map tower-operation choices and their effects on servants, annexes, and resources |
| Planning stats | Wit and Charisma are planning notes for administration and persuasion; log uncertainty separately from stats |
| Suggested anchors | Gwendan, Angelica, and Dullahan are useful comparison picks when available |
| Best traits | Problem solving, diplomacy, route knowledge, and careful judgment |
| Main risk | Treating flavor servant dialogue as a confirmed mechanical unlock before testing |

## Steps / Route Fit

1. Log each servant, Intendant scene, annex reference, and tower-operation problem as it appears.
2. Before choosing, note current resources, faction state, roster injuries, and available annexes.
3. Assign knights by visible requirement or scene logic, then record whether traits alter trust, discipline, recovery, or access.
4. Separate administrative flavor from confirmed system changes such as new positions, resource shifts, or unlocked options.
5. Rewind around unclear choices to test whether a different knight or ruling changes the same servant outcome.
6. Split confirmed servant or Intendant events into separate quest records once their branches are stable.

## FAQ

### Is the Intendant route a combat route?

No. Treat it as a tower-management route unless a specific event shows combat or quest assignment requirements.

### Can servant choices unlock annexes?

They may, but this page should keep unlock language cautious until a choice reliably produces a system change.

### Which stats matter for servant events?

Wit and Charisma are useful planning categories. Treat uncertain outcomes as route notes unless the active save shows a stat label.

### When should a servant event get its own page?

Create a dedicated page when the event has a stable name, clear requirement, known outcome, and repeatable route consequence.
