## Quick Answer

The full campaign route should be managed as a route ledger, not a single fixed walkthrough. Track each act, every major faction shift, recruitable knight branch, servant or annex unlock, and romance flag before committing to claims about endings or hard locks.

## Campaign Overview

| Main risk | Treating one successful run as proof of a universal campaign route |

## Steps / Route Fit

1. Start a campaign ledger before Act 0 ends: record roster, faction state, tower access, known quests, and unresolved route hooks.
2. Split every confirmed milestone into a child page when it has a stable quest name, visible requirement, and repeatable outcome.
3. Mark broad stat advice as provisional unless the quest interface shows the exact requirement.
4. Track when faction, romance, servant, and knight recruitment choices begin to compete with each other.
5. Use rewind tests around major decisions to separate soft consequences from true route locks.
6. At each act transition, summarize carried flags: loyalty, injuries, faction standing, treasury pressure, annex access, and recruited knights.

## FAQ

### Is there one best full campaign route?

Not yet. The safest route is a well-documented campaign ledger that lets you compare branches instead of assuming one path is optimal.

### Which choices should I track first?

Track faction shifts, recruitment decisions, romance signals, servant or annex changes, and any quest failure that affects loyalty or future availability.

### Should placeholder stats be used for routing?

Use them only as planning hints. Exact recommendations should come from visible quest requirements or repeated in-game results.

### When is a campaign claim ready to publish?

Publish a firm claim only after the same decision produces the same effect across repeated runs or is confirmed by official text.
