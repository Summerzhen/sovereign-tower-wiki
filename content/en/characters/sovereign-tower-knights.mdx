## Why Your Knights Make or Break Your Reign

In Sovereign Tower, your knights are the backbone of your rule. Assign the wrong knight to a quest and you could lose them forever—yes, permanently. Understanding how Sovereign Tower knights work, from their stats to their hidden attributes, is the difference between a thriving kingdom and a round table with empty seats. This guide covers every known knight from the demo, explains the stat and affinity systems, and offers practical advice for keeping your knights alive and loyal.

## How Knight Recruitment Works

Knights join your court through the audience phase, where characters arrive at the tower requesting an audience with the sovereign. Each cycle brings new visitors—some seeking to serve as knights, others as servants with their own functions. You choose whether to welcome them, and those accepted as knights take a seat at the round table.

Once recruited, you assign knights to quests during the round table phase by dragging their portrait onto available quests. Assignments lock only when you end the cycle, so you can shuffle them freely before committing.

### Key Recruitment Details

- **Audience requests** can be used to invite back characters you previously turned away or asked to return later.
- **Servants and knights serve different roles.** Servants like Karina the blacksmith unlock rooms and shops, while knights handle quests directly.
- **You can recruit multiple knights per cycle**, but your options depend on who appears for audience—some cycles may offer more candidates than others.

### Lady Angelica of Clovermont

Angelica is Sir Ireland's niece and your first knight. She's young, idealistic, and genuinely kind—she'll ask to bring a cat to the tower because the cold scares her. Her high agility and luck make her surprisingly effective on physical quests, but her extremely low wit (1) means she struggles with negotiation or puzzle-solving tasks. In the demo, she failed a cheese-related negotiation quest but thrived when dealing with a war goose, where her friendly nature turned a bizarre situation into a diplomatic success.

### Sir Gabberto of Noaned

Gabberto is a monster-hunting specialist who claims expertise with trolls, dragons, spiders, and even "thirsty chickens." His 7 strength makes him a natural fit for combat-heavy assignments. However, player experience shows he can suffer unexpected outcomes—including death—if assigned to quests that don't suit his attributes. He's the youngest son in a massive family with 12 siblings and 78 cousins, which drives his eagerness to make a name for himself. He writes letters to his little sister Bertha about his adventures.

### Sir Gwendon of Vidor

Gwendon arrives declaring himself the greatest knight in all the land. His 7 charisma is his standout stat, backed by decent strength and agility. However, community reports from the demo reveal a hidden vulnerability: he has a fear of the dark, which the player can discover through gossip investigation via Alwina in the Intendency room. His aunt, Baroness Gwendelyn Barilles of Villa Darin, is an influential noble who visits the tower and isn't shy about reminding everyone of her nephew's brilliance—or criticizing the state of your tower.

## Knight Stats and Quest Assignment Strategy

Each knight has six core stats that determine quest performance: Strength, Agility, Charisma, Luck, Magic, and Wit. When a knight levels up, you allocate one point to a stat of your choice.

| Stat | Affects | Priority For |
|------|---------|-------------|
| Strength | Combat, physical challenges | Monster slaying, duels |
| Agility | Speed, climbing, dodging | Retrieval, chase quests |
| Charisma | Negotiation, persuasion | Diplomatic, social quests |
| Luck | Random outcomes, unexpected events | Any quest with variable results |
| Magic | Mystical challenges | Spell-related, relic quests |
| Wit | Problem-solving, deduction | Puzzles, investigation quests |

### Reading Quest Requirements

Quests display a difficulty range and stat requirements. Pay close attention to these indicators:

- **Difficulty ratings** (e.g., 0–1, 1–2, 2–4) suggest the overall challenge level and how many knights may be needed.
- **Damage risk indicators** show how likely knights are to take armor damage during the quest.
- **Quest descriptions contain clues** about which stats matter. A quest involving negotiation needs charisma; a quest about climbing needs agility.

### The Critical Lesson: Match Stats to Quests

The demo delivers a harsh lesson in quest assignment. When Sir Gabberto was sent on the "Magpie's Stolen Relic" quest—a retrieval task requiring agility—he died from an unexpected outcome. When the player used the Demon Power to rewind time and sent Angelica (8 agility) instead, she succeeded and even earned a donkey mount. Gabberto, reassigned to the goose quest, survived despite disliking the assignment.

**Takeaway:** High stats in the relevant category matter more than knight preference. A knight who dislikes a quest may lose affinity, but a knight with wrong stats may not come back at all.

## Affinity, Preferences, and Keeping Knights Happy

Every knight has an affinity score reflecting how they feel about you, the sovereign. This system has real consequences.

### How Affinity Works

- **Increases** through successful quests, flirtatious dialogue choices, and assigning knights to quests they prefer.
- **Decreases** through major quest failures and assigning knights to quests they dislike.
- **High affinity** unlocks new dialogue options and deeper character interactions.
- **Low affinity** can cause a knight to leave the round table permanently.

### Knight Preferences

Each knight has quest type preferences visible in the glossary. These preferences don't affect quest performance—only affinity. For example, Sir Gabberto has a noted preference for "Relic Discovery" quests. Sending him on a beast hunt instead may lower his affinity even if he succeeds.

### Balancing Performance and Happiness

The tension between optimal stat matching and knight preference creates meaningful decisions:

- **When stats align with preference**, assign freely—you get both success and affinity.
- **When stats conflict with preference**, prioritize stats to avoid quest failure or knight death, then repair affinity through dialogue choices later.
- **Flirting and kind dialogue choices** can offset affinity losses from unfavorable quest assignments.

## The Demon Power: Your Safety Net

Hidden in the crypts beneath the tower, a demon offers a powerful rewind ability. The Demon Power lets you return to any previous cycle in the current act while retaining all acquired knowledge—including knight tags, preferences, and quest requirements.

### What Carries Over on Rewind

- Knight attribute tags and discovered preferences
- Quest stat requirements you've learned
- Knowledge of which assignments lead to bad outcomes

### What Changes on Rewind

- The audience phase may offer different characters
- Dialogue options and events may shift
- Your affinity and relationship progress resets to that cycle's state

This mechanic encourages experimentation. If a knight dies or a quest fails catastrophically, you can rewind with better information and make smarter assignments—though you may lose access to specific recruits who appeared in the original timeline.

## Managing Knight Damage and Equipment

Knights can take damage during quests, which affects their armor. Damaged armor can be repaired at the Forge, operated by Karina the blacksmith. The Forge also sells relics and equipment that may improve knight performance on future quests.

- **Check armor status** before assigning knights to high-difficulty quests.
- **Repair armor proactively** at the Forge between cycles rather than waiting until a knight is critically damaged.
- **Mounts earned from quests** (like the donkey Paw or the noble steed Enba) appear in the equipment tab and can provide cycle bonuses.

## FAQ

### Can Sovereign Tower knights die permanently?

Yes. Player experience from the demo confirms that knights can die from unexpected quest outcomes. Sir Gabberto died when assigned to a quest with mismatched stats, and the death was permanent within that playthrough. The only way to reverse this is using the Demon Power to rewind to a previous cycle.

### How do I increase a knight's affinity?

Assign knights to quests matching their preferences, choose kind or flirtatious dialogue options during conversations, and achieve critical or great successes on quests. Avoid major failures and repeatedly assigning knights to quests they dislike, as low affinity can cause them to abandon the round table.

### What happens if I use the Demon Power to rewind time?

You return to a previous cycle keeping all meta-knowledge—knight tags, preferences, and quest requirements—but the audience phase may offer different characters than before. This means you could lose access to recruits from the original timeline while gaining new ones. The Demon Power is tracked in the Sovereign menu, so use it strategically.

### Which Sovereign Tower knights are best for early quests?

Lady Angelica excels on agility-based and social quests due to her 8 agility and friendly personality. Sir Gabberto is ideal for combat and monster quests with his 7 strength. For quests requiring negotiation or charm, Sir Gwendon's 7 charisma makes him the strongest option—just be aware of his hidden fear of the dark when selecting assignments.
