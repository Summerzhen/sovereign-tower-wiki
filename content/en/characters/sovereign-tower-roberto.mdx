## How Time Travel Affects Characters in Sovereign Tower

The time travel system in Sovereign Tower is not just a gimmick—it fundamentally reshapes your relationship with every character in the game. Understanding how it works is critical for anyone trying to experience all the game's character content.

### Knowledge Carries Over

When you turn back time, your character retains everything learned in the erased timeline. This means:

- You know which knights have hidden negative traits
- You know the outcomes of quests before sending knights on them
- You know who committed crimes and why
- Entirely new dialogue options and decisions open up based on your future knowledge

### The Cost of Changing Timelines

Erasing a timeline is not without consequences. When you prevent a knight's murder, you also erase every event that flowed from that crime—some of which may have been positive. You might bring back one character while losing access to another entirely.

This creates a genuine ethical weight to every time travel decision. The game never lets you forget that you are not just fixing a problem—you are destroying an entire sequence of events and replacing it with something unknown.

### Practical Tips for Character Discovery via Time Travel

- **Before rewinding, take mental notes** of which characters you want to preserve or investigate further
- **Use your first timeline as a scouting run**—learn knight traits, uncover crimes, and map relationships before committing to changes
- **Don't assume the "good" timeline is obvious**—the game deliberately avoids clear right answers
- **Talk to the demon regularly**—it often hints at what you could change, though its motives are suspect

## Maximizing Your Character Relationships

Whether you are looking for Roberto or simply trying to build the strongest court possible, these strategies will help you get the most out of Sovereign Tower's character systems.

### Learn Before You Assign

Never send a knight on a critical quest without knowing their traits. The cost of sending an advisor to spy is almost always lower than the cost of a failed quest. Early investment in intelligence pays dividends throughout the game.

### Balance Satisfaction and Effectiveness

A knight who succeeds on quests but hates every assignment will eventually become a problem. Pay attention to preferences, not just ability scores. Sometimes the slightly less capable knight who enjoys the task is the better long-term choice.

Sovereign Tower does not punish you for being a terrible ruler—it punishes you for being indecisive. If you need to send a ruthless knight to steal, murder, or cheat, the game gives you that option. Your citizens and fellow rulers will judge you, but judgment is not the same as failure.
